0 m. The surface

Above, red dirt. Below, a labyrinth.

Andamooka, South Australia, the late 1980s. The sun flattens everything on top. Underneath, hand-cut shafts run in every direction. Opal is fortune and freedom here, and everyone owes someone for the chance to dig.

By day the town works its claims. By night it settles its debts.

6 m. The Ghost

Taj takes a percentage.

Part criminal, part folk hero, Taj is the town's unofficial power broker. He settles disputes, takes his cut, and is easy to like. That's what makes him dangerous.

14 m. The Father

One find from everything.

The father is a charismatic miner chasing the big strike. He drinks. He answers to nobody, though he leans on Taj more than he would ever say.

22 m. The Son

A boy with two heroes.

The son idolises his father, and he idolises Taj. Then a girl he cares about is harmed by an older man the town prefers not to see. The boy goes out into the diggings to face him. The man doesn't come back.

30 m. The rupture

Because he knows me!

The father hides what happened. Taj knows, and takes the boy's side. Father and son split over it, and the mother sees the damage and has no power to stop it.

Andamooka

A town that lives under itself

Andamooka sits roughly 600 km north of Adelaide, in opal country. Opal was first found here in 1930, and the town grew around the digging.

People live in dugouts cut into the hillside, work claims by hand, and noodle through mullock heaps hoping for a flash of colour. The film is set in the late 1980s and early 1990s, when a big find still felt one shaft away.

Built from what was lying around

Local stone, tin and brush fencing, a bench of rough opal outside the door. Miners' huts in the 1950s were made to be lived in and left. A lot of Andamooka still looks like this.

100 million years ago

Before the town, a sea

This country was once the floor and shoreline of an inland sea. Silica-rich water seeped into the cracks and hollows of the sediment and set, slowly, as opal. The colour the miners chase is what's left of that water.
